## S5 Historical experiments retained as diagnostic context

### S5.1 Targets, selection and frozen predictions

These experiments precede the source audit reported in the article. They are preserved to expose failures and the development history, rather than used as evidence of a new predictive method. Their administrative target y and three-state historical evidence reference z must be kept separate. A logistic-regression classifier trained to y is not a matched evidence-audit comparator to a language model instructed to abstain when a necessary condition is unestablished. Binary A/B comparisons, native three-state outputs and direct JSON generation are therefore not pooled in the new functional analysis.

The 86 historical pairs are grouped using common ruling, annotated rule family or near-duplicate descriptions. Canonicalization removes the described-in locator, lowercases and normalizes nonalphanumeric characters. Character TF–IDF similarities of at least 0.87 for rules or 0.90 for facts add links. Transitive components yield 45 groups in all 86 pairs and 41 in the compatible-code subset. Five stratified outer group folds use seed 20261007. Classical preprocessing and regularization selection occur within training data, with three inner group folds. Metrics use pooled held-out predictions and are then restricted to the named reporting subset. The exact-code subset is a composition sensitivity, not an independent test corpus.

The historical interval procedure uses 2,000 percentile resamples of linked groups, holding predictions fixed. Paired contrasts share the same resampled groups. Draws lacking a necessary class have undefined balanced accuracy and are excluded with their valid count retained. This is conditional uncertainty within the selected corpus; it includes neither unknown selection probabilities nor refitting uncertainty. Additional grouped seeds, cross-office training and chronological splits remain sensitivity analyses. For word-plus-character models, the original and additional-seed primary balanced accuracies are 56.5%, 58.9%, 58.9% and 56.3%. Group-purged source transfer yields 33.9% for HQ-to-NY and 40.9% for NY-to-HQ, while the pre-2023 chronological model attains 37.5% on 22 later compatible-code pairs. These shifted comparisons combine changes in training size, class composition and text distribution.

The original AI condition coding and a subsequent case-level reading agreed on 90.4% of cases, with Cohen's kappa 0.814. The second pass excluded administrative disposition, source, date and agency reasoning from its immediate input, but had prior project exposure. Both passes shared model lineage. The second was not a complete condition-span annotation, and the shared relay-rating error illustrates that agreement does not validate an interpretation. The final operational reconciliation retains both initial readings and subsequent corrections. None is independently validated legal gold.

### S5.2 Interface failures and restricted scores

The compact model's orthogonal audit crosses six A/B/C meaning assignments with six display orders on nine ordinary-attribute items, yielding 324 executions. It returns the intended semantic state on 115 executions (35.5%). Letter counts are A 210, B 28 and C 86, while display-position counts are first 131, second 64 and third 129. This is interface sensitivity rather than a demonstrated account of internal model behavior. The older nine-item controls produce Qwen14 scores of 6/9 under ABC and 7/9 under JSON; Granite produces 5/9 and 4/9. These configurations were not qualified by the new gate and do not represent a general limit of the checkpoints or of decomposition.

The old Granite extraction stage asks for literal-quoted requirements and an AND/OR/NOT tree. Its cache contains 55 unique rule scopes; only twelve pass the fixed structural and quote checks. The primary collection consequently has 61/73 invalid cases, compared with twelve valid cases. Output limits and truncated inputs do not explain these failures. A validator certifying a tree or a source quote does not certify completeness or exception scope. The new functional study supplies a checklist and does not claim to repair or outperform this automatic extractor.

### S5.3 Numeric execution and representation interventions

The historical local test selects one quantitative clause from each of 60 official descriptions: 28 two-sided, 29 upper-bound and three lower-bound conditions. Eight slots per rule cover interior, boundary, outside, conversion, missing-measurement and straddling-interval behavior. Other requirements are satisfied by construction. The source-coded reference uses the printed values and exact arithmetic. Ambiguous alternatives and a source-unit anomaly are excluded; approximately stated values without a tolerance do not become exact measurements. A numeric interval represents uncertainty about one attribute, not a pool of distinct products or rating roles.

The frozen executor obtains 466/480 correct slots (97.1%), 430/443 distinct rule–fact specifications and 422/435 distinct exact model inputs. The 60 source rules reduce to 59 literal clauses; resampling those literal clauses gives a 93.6–100.0% interval. Of 120 conversion slots, 112 change representation and eight are identity controls; 108/112 changes and all eight controls are correct. These results validate a local execution capability with standardized facts, rather than source extraction or complete legal applicability.

The real-case intervention uses 43 numeric conditions in 33 pairs from 16 linked groups. The source-coded relay adapter explicitly retains discrete per-product ratings; this binding is unavailable to the original automatic extractor. Internal match with reference rules and fields is consequently a consistency check partly defined by that same representation. For reproducible comparison with the original evaluator, not_parsed is normalized to unknown. Removing the fifteen-pair largest family does not remove the extraction loss.

### S5.4 Guard policy and hypothetical review costs

The frozen guard corrects no administrative binary error in the primary comparison. For Qwen4 it changes native coverage from 67/73 to 47/73. Answered administrative disagreement changes from 34.3% to 31.9%, with a paired difference interval of −9.0 to 4.6 percentage points; this does not establish improved retained error. Among the five historical refutations, three correct Qwen4 negatives become one correct negative after fusion. The losses occur for H318593_2 and N307161. The former has a 6,000 lb capacity above 550 kg; the latter's stated dimensions of 140 × 150 × 86 mm differ from a candidate description measuring 148 mm in length, 43 mm in width and 335 mm in height. That description states dimensions, not upper-bound ceilings. Unparsed numeric output triggered an unknown override. This is a fusion choice, distinct from the signed-conjunction rule under which a verified refutation remains decisive.

No comparison treats an unverified language-model negative as established source evidence. Whether to refer such a negative is a separate review policy. Likewise, referral is an action, not a correct legal answer. The five-refutation class is small: one changed case alters its recall by twenty percentage points and three-class macro recall by 6.67 points. Fine model rankings are therefore inappropriate.

The guarded Qwen4 example has five false grants, ten false denials and 26 referrals relative to administrative labels. With false-grant cost cg, false-denial cost cd and referral cost cr, its hypothetical mean loss is (5cg + 10cd + 26cr) / 73. Review-all loss is cr, so the guard is preferable only when cr > (5cg + 10cd) / 47. For cg = 5 and cd = 1, the threshold is 0.74468. At cr = 0.5, guarded loss is 0.65753 and review-all loss 0.5. This assumes that referral resolves every case correctly at fixed cost. It omits residual review error, capacity limits and actual legal or monetary consequences. The preserved complete cost grid below is a sensitivity calculation, not an economic benefit estimate.
