# Supplementary material

*Auditing evidence construction in legal AI evaluation*

## S1 Materials, targets and reproducibility

This supplement accompanies *Auditing evidence construction in legal AI evaluation*. It documents the source audit, inventory reading, qualified functional demonstration and complete historical diagnostics. The article's principal contribution is an inspectable relationship between a public source, the statements admitted to a model input and the condition represented by a reference state. The accompanying records permit an alternative admission or interpretation to be examined without changing the retained source.

Table S1 Resource map and target boundaries

| Component | Material supplied | Target or permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Source audit, Section S2 | 208 condition records, 73 pair records, 67 source manifests, exact locations and restoration records | Evidence in a versioned public text under a stated policy; no independent expert reference |
| Inventory, Section S3 | 397 row readings, 1,096 coarse source-anchored groups and case links | Structure in the dated index and a conservative textual intersection |
| Qualified demonstration, Section S4 | Development and qualification cases, frozen inputs, prompts, raw responses, reference correction and analyses | Controlled evidence tracking with a supplied rule representation |

| Reproducibility package | Versioned scripts, manifests, official-source snapshots and a score verification program | Recalculation of retained results; a fresh inference run additionally requires model weights and the recorded inference environment |

The administrative outcome y is the disposition stated by CBP. Historical evidence coding z records whether an admitted excerpt supports, refutes or leaves unresolved the supplied candidate. The strict audit further distinguishes direct-text states, interpretation options and logical structures that cannot be evaluated as an outer conjunction. These targets are not interchangeable. An administrative outcome can have evidence outside the retained input, and a conservative unknown may withhold a determinate administrative answer without contradicting it.

The discovery sequence of 2,482 retrieved documents, 102 candidates, 86 constructed pairs and 73 compatible-code pairs describes iterative purposive construction. It does not imply an exhaustive screen with known inclusion probabilities. The preserved retrieval material cannot reconstruct every contemporaneous screening decision. Public rulings are already selected and written with knowledge of their outcomes. Removing a holding or restoring a descriptive attribute does not turn them into original applicant dossiers.

All new source and inventory interpretations are AI-assisted readings. Parallel review and exact quotation checks improve inspectability but do not substitute for independent trade-law validation. The available assistant identity is the GPT-6 family through Codex; an immutable provider snapshot was not exposed. Reviewers could access prior project materials and administrative outcomes. Historical agreement statistics therefore concern those coding procedures, not independent legal validity. The local model responses in Section S4 are separate experimental observations generated by the recorded Qwen3-14B configuration.

The package preserves frozen materials and corrections as separate versions. The controlled frame-species erratum changes a condition reference and admissible evidence sets without changing inputs or re-running predictions. The formal analysis reports both reference versions and a complete family-exclusion sensitivity. Superseded input-pilot responses, qualification request failures, earlier format failures and numeric development repairs remain available. No seed or replay converts previously inspected material into an unseen validation set.

Verification has two levels. Source checks confirm snapshot identity, literal quotation locations, row coverage and declared policy arithmetic. Response checks reconstruct whitelisted prompts, parse retained raw JSON and independently recount the stated metrics. Neither level establishes the physical truth of a product claim, completeness of a legal decomposition or authority of an interpretive choice. A fresh model run is a separate operation from deterministic recomputation of stored outputs.

The records concern published legal materials rather than recruited participants. No study of professional review time, importer behavior or filing outcomes was conducted. The hypothetical cost grid in Section S5 is explicitly assumption dependent and includes a review-all comparator. Its units are normalized losses rather than observed money or staff hours.

## S2 Source audit and annotation lineage

The source audit covers 73 historical product–exclusion pairs, 208 retained condition units and 67 official CBP rulings. Its purpose is to make the relationship between a public source, an admitted factual input and a condition state inspectable. It neither reconstructs the original importer dossier nor supplies independently validated legal answers. The candidate description remains the exclusion quoted in each ruling; it is not replaced by a superficially similar description in the January 2024 USTR index. Historical inputs and coding remain available alongside the audited records.

### S2.1 Records, statement roles and source locations

Every retained condition, its complete frozen factual input, cited evidence and product–attribute assignment was reread. All 47 historically unknown pairs received additional examination of relevant factual and analytical passages throughout their complete public rulings. The remaining pairs received complete excerpt review and source-span checks, with targeted context examination for numerical and refuted conditions. The assembly program checks locations and coverage; the product descriptions, attribute assignments and interpretive distinctions were assigned through AI-assisted reading rather than inferred by that program.

Table S2.1 lists the principal fields in `source_audit/condition_audit_long.jsonl`. These records preserve the original and later operational coding, rather than overwriting either with the strict audit state. A condition identifier denotes an inherited review unit. It does not certify that the unit is atomic or that the decomposition exhausts the legal description.

| Identity and rule | Case, ruling and condition identifiers; literal candidate quote and location | Links the unit to its actual historical scope |
| Governed relation | Product or component, attribute, quantifier and measurement scope | Distinguishes the target from other products, parts or ratings |
| Logic | Signed requirement, logical role, local alternatives, exception or illustrative status | Prevents negative wording and local alternatives from becoming mandatory positive conjuncts |

The source-location checks validate 455 continuous span instances and two noncontiguous quotation records: 208 rule locations, 231 continuous source locations corresponding to 228 frozen evidence quotations, and 16 restoration or contextual records. Repeated quotations can contribute more than one checked instance; 455 is not a count of independent observations. The two composite records, N314745_self and N314745_pedal, concatenate selected product paragraphs from the same ruling. Their members have separate continuous locations, rather than an invented continuous source span.

Character offsets refer to the unmodified decoded official text. Recorded whitespace or typographic normalization permits matching a retained quotation while preserving the original characters at its source location. Source attribution distinguishes party descriptions, laboratory findings, agency-stated attributes, definitions, application reasoning and outcomes. A descriptive clause in the analysis can be admitted without admitting the applicability conclusion surrounding it. Conversely, a party's proposed legal characterization does not become an established physical attribute merely because it appears under a factual heading. Successful location checks establish provenance, not legal or empirical validity.

### S2.2 Reference tiers and state lineage

The audit assigns 132 units to the direct tier, 45 to the interpretive tier, 19 to insufficient evidence and 12 to disputed evidence. Of the direct units, 130 are scoreable requirements and two are nonbinding: an illustrative “including” list and a “whether or not” permission. Direct relations allow explicit textual attributes, ordinary lexical equivalence, simple comparisons, unit conversion and discrete membership checks. They do not imply independent measurement of the reported attribute. Interpretive relations require an additional definition, category, functional equivalence or scope mapping. Insufficient records lack an admitted attribute in the relevant input. Disputed records retain conflicting assertions or unresolved interpretation, object or quantifier choices.

Tier and state are distinct. Tier assignment describes the evidentiary relation in the frozen input and remains recorded after restoration. The strict excerpt has 125 supported, five refuted, 76 unknown and two non-applicable units. Admitting the directly recoverable attributes yields 129 supported, five refuted, 72 unknown and two non-applicable units. These states must not be merged with the historical case reference of 21 supported, five refuted and 47 unknown pairs: they use different admission and aggregation policies.

### S2.4 Direct restoration and its limits

Four condition relations in three rulings change from strict unknown to support. H330938 c4 receives the statement that submitted documentation describes cables imported without connectors. H322923 c2 receives the explicit four-wheel description. H340899 c3 and c4 receive an assertion of self-contained electric motors with power not exceeding 1,500 W. These relations use three distinct added source sentences. Their original locations and statement attribution are retained in `source_audit/recovered_attribute_spans.jsonl`.

Only three of the four conditions were historically unknown. H340899 c3 had already been inferred as supported in the historical reference; its before-state is unknown under the stricter direct-text policy. Accordingly, the experiment measures changed availability of explicit attribute evidence, not correction of four historical unknown labels. Whole-case uncertainty can persist after restoration. Other passages are retained separately when their use requires additional decisions: H341677's denial of network communication requires a communications reading of “wireless,” while H314080's width and areal-mass intervals repeat the protesting party's proposed exclusion description. The strict policy retains these as attributed claims with an explicit acceptance option. Party attribution and absence of laboratory retesting are not general exclusion criteria.

### S2.6 Five traceable evidence chains

The complete chains in `source_audit/case_chains.json` include every condition, admitted quote and locator for the following five rulings.

**H330938.** The analytical paragraph beginning “Lastly, the submitted documentation” explicitly supplies connector absence. This corrects the earlier claim that the complete ruling lacked connector-specific evidence. Monopolar topology and the aluminum-core/copper-shield distinction remain separate interpretive switches; resolving c4 does not resolve them.

**H318593.** The product-description paragraph gives dimensions of 61 × 30.2 × 15.2 cm “exclusive of handle.” It cannot establish the unqualified complete-article ceiling in H318593_1. The same paragraph gives a 6,000 lb lifting capacity, approximately 2,721.55 kg, which directly contradicts the separate 550 kg ceiling in H318593_2. Article dimensions, article mass and supported load are distinct attributes.

**N311039.** Separate product paragraphs locate coil/contact voltage alternatives of 12/277, 110/277, 48/277 and 48/277 V. The coil ratings are AC for the first product and DC for the other three; contact ratings are AC. Products one, three and four fail the interval (60, 250] V under either rating-role interpretation. Under the declared all-listed-products quantifier, the condition is refuted without pooling values into an uncertainty interval. Contact ratings of 30, 40, 40 and 40 A independently satisfy the current threshold.

**H333371.** The party-composition and laboratory-report passages respectively give 48% and approximately 76% textile content. Both are visible. Source restoration cannot resolve their conflict; the competing priority policies remain explicit.

**H333673.** Component descriptions and the analytical sentence beginning “Out of the four ancillary sensors” identify passive infrared light-switching functionality in the front-light sensor. They do not settle whether the exclusion requires one qualifying component or a universal apparatus-level property. ### S2.7 Validation boundary

The annotations and this audit are AI assisted, without independent human or domain-expert validation. The auditors had prior exposure to administrative outcomes and earlier project materials; this is not a new blind annotation. Outcomes were not criteria for selecting evidence states or interpretation packages. Shared model lineage permits shared semantic errors despite successful mechanical checks. The contribution of these records is inspectability and recalculation: readers can contest a quoted relation, change a declared mapping or withhold an assertion while preserving its source and annotation history.

## S3 Structure and coverage of the exclusion inventory

### S3.1 Inventory identity and purpose

The inventory contains 397 entries transcribed from a USTR index dated 17 January 2024. Its purpose is to document the range of structures present in that version and the limited intersection between the index and the historical ruling collection. It is not a sampling frame for every exclusion previously issued, a current list of legally effective exclusions, or a replacement for the candidate wording quoted in an individual ruling. The original transcription is preserved. Each audit record retains its index, rule identifier, aliases, tariff code, source wording, version and source locator. An alias or similar description is treated as a retrieval lead unless the specified matching criterion is satisfied.

All inventory indices below refer to the zero-based JSON field, from 0 to 396, rather than PDF row numbers; index 13 is therefore the fourteenth inventory record. Rule identifiers remain the primary legal-source locators.

The format distinction is consequential. There are 41 single-code entries, two entries containing date-dependent code transitions, and 354 product descriptions. A code-only entry has no additional product-description groups in this inventory, but it still has a classification gate and, where stated, a date branch. Its group count of zero therefore does not mean that no legal requirements apply. Conversely, the presence of a product description does not establish that its requirements are difficult, quantitative or fully specified.

### S3.2 Reading procedure and representational granularity

Every entry was read individually by an AI research agent. The resulting silver annotations record the target level, such as article, component or assembly; visible alternatives and exceptions; measurement attributes; and references requiring external definitions. The record also separates surface structure from unresolved interpretation. Explicit row-level choices and source hashes are retained, rather than inferring a completed semantic review from a successful parser run. No legal professional independently validated these inventory labels.

A second pass selected exact source-anchored coarse requirement groups for all 354 product descriptions. Thirty initial readings used explicitly selected quotations; the remaining 324 used individually specified start anchors in the original wording. This produced 1,096 groups. The anchors were chosen after reading each scope and were not generated by counting punctuation or conjunction words. Each group has a literal quotation and character offsets. All 397 entries have a recorded coarse group count, including the 43 code-only records with a separately represented taxonomic or temporal gate.

The counting convention intentionally stops above the level of atomic legal predicates. Product categories, materials, functions, physical configurations and measurement requirements can form separate groups. A categorical alternative, constituent inventory, dimension vector for one object, or conditionally applicable standard may remain within one group. Optional modifiers can remain in that group's wording without becoming positive requirements. Across groups the reading is conjunctive, but internal alternatives, exclusions and guards retain their original scope. For example, a component list containing a burner and a stand is not counted as two independent products. This convention makes the source reading inspectable; it does not assign an ontology-independent number of necessary conditions.

Atomic condition counts, fully resolved external definitions and complete formal quantifier representations are consequently not asserted. The 1,096 total describes this particular coarse representation and should not be interpreted as a legal complexity score. It also should not be compared directly with the 208 historical condition units, which were constructed for a different collection and purpose. Inspecting the quoted groups is more informative than comparing their counts without regard to what each group contains.

### S3.3 Object and logical scope

Several entries illustrate why a numerical or lexical inventory would be insufficient. The die description at index 13 limits the width or length of the sheets being processed, rather than the dimensions of the die. The alternative is retained as stated. At index 95, the motor's outside diameter, housing length and shaft length concern different objects. At index 145, input voltage and frequency are distinguished from two alternative output voltages; combining these values into a single interval would erase their roles.

Other requirements concern composition or conditional application. The trailer description at index 189 attaches compliance with ASTM F1975 to trailers designated for carrying children. It is not an unconditional requirement for every trailer. Index 195 combines three wind cups with bearings, a fan and one or more solar panels. Index 216 assigns different dimensions to two separate liner pieces. Index 218 constrains the reel carrying a lighting strip, rather than the strip itself. Index 338 uses shape-dependent dimensions for rectangular and circular tracking devices alongside shared thickness and mass limits. These readings preserve the relationships that a later executable representation would need to justify.

External identifiers require a different treatment. DIN 42802 identifies a connector standard, while the FDA Class 1 and NNI references describe a regulatory status. Their digits are not measurement thresholds. Similarly, a stated weighing capacity cannot substitute for an article's own mass. Preserving these distinctions helps identify what evidence or incorporated definitions an applicability review would need, without claiming that the inventory itself supplies them.

### S3.4 Ambiguities and mechanical checks

Thirteen entries have an explicit unresolved marker for an interpretive, binding or source issue. This is a recorded review outcome, not an exhaustive estimate of ambiguity prevalence. Examples include an unclear attachment of a weight limit, an unspecified concentration basis, and a shaft measurement whose dimensional role is not named. The shelving entry at index 211 states an upper width of 183 m. The same wording was confirmed in the official PDF; it was preserved rather than silently changed to centimetres. The fireplace entry at index 155 states 5,000 BTUs without an explicit hourly basis, so the audit does not automatically treat it as power.

Mechanical validation checked 3,260 exact source spans across retrieval candidates, structural markers, coarse groups and code/date gates. It also verified record identity, source hashes and coverage links. These checks establish correspondence to the retained source, not semantic correctness or the validity of an external legal interpretation. In particular, a group can be accurately quoted while its object binding remains unresolved.

### S3.5 Coverage of the ruling collection

Conservative normalized description matching linked 21 of the 86 product–exclusion pairs to 16 distinct index entries. Sixteen matched pairs belong to the 73-pair compatible-code collection. Recorded note aliases linked 11 cases and 11 entries, all within that textual intersection. Unconfirmed similar descriptions were retained separately and were not counted as matches.

Thus the confirmed intersection is 16 of 397 inventory entries, not a demonstration of model coverage across the full index. Matching also does not establish historical version equivalence. The inventory supports a transparent account of representational variety and coverage limits, while the ruling-specific clause remains the source for each historical applicability audit.

## S4 Qualified interfaces and controlled evidence auditing

### S4.1 Scope and independent protocol qualification

This revision evaluates evidence state z rather than administrative disposition y. A condition is supported when the supplied facts establish it, refuted when they contradict it, and unknown when neither relation is established. Unknown is therefore an input-evidence state, not a negative legal ruling. The task supplies the relevant condition representation and tests evidence tracking, justified follow-up and three-valued aggregation. It does not evaluate discovery of every legal prerequisite or complete autonomous interpretation of an exclusion.

The configuration was developed and qualified on ordinary-object examples separate from the customs materials. Four development inputs tested a green bead with positive, contradictory and missing color evidence, and an opaque tube whose weight was unstated. These inputs contained five conditions. The frozen qualification set contained 24 inputs and 27 conditions: direct material and shape attributes, strict and interval bounds, connector negation, frame-versus-screw object binding, net-versus-gross measurement basis, and conjunctions with decisive contradiction or missing evidence. Qualification had eight supported, eight refuted and eight unknown whole-input states.

The model order was fixed as Qwen3-14B Q4_K_M followed, only if necessary, by Granite-3.3-8B Q4_K_M. A configuration had to achieve at least 23/24 correct whole-input decisions, no invalid, incomplete or truncated output, no unsupported affirmative decision, and correct decisive refutation in the presence of another unknown condition. The checklist additionally had to achieve at least 95% condition-state agreement and request nothing after that decisive refutation. Direct output has no condition or request fields, so corresponding checklist-only thresholds were not imputed to it. Qwen3-14B satisfied the fixed gate; the fallback was not needed. No formal customs or controlled diagnostic outcome selected the checkpoint or prompt.

Both direct and checklist interfaces obtained 4/4 development decisions; the checklist obtained 5/5 condition states. Both obtained 24/24 qualification decisions, and the checklist obtained 27/27 condition states. All qualification outputs were structurally valid and complete, with no truncation or unsupported affirmative case decision. Qualification nevertheless revealed a follow-up failure. In Q_negation_3, the lead was required to have no connectors, while the only fact gave its length as 4 m. The checklist correctly marked connector absence and the whole input unknown but returned an empty request list. It therefore retrieved seven of eight necessary qualification requests, with no unnecessary request, and matched 23/24 exact request sets. General request recall was not a frozen acceptance threshold; this failure was retained rather than used to revise the prompt after qualification. Passing these limited checks does not establish legal competence.

### S4.2 Local configuration and inference controls

Inference used the publisher-provided Qwen3-14B-GGUF checkpoint, file Qwen3-14B-Q4_K_M.gguf, with its pinned repository revision and verified official weight digest retained in the external manifest. The local llama.cpp server reported version 0.6.0-dev, build 11516, built with Clang 20.1.8 for Windows x86_64. The runner used Python 3.12.10 on an AMD Ryzen 9 8945HS host with eight physical cores, sixteen logical processors and approximately 32 GB installed memory. No GPU layers were offloaded. Six threads were used for both prefill and inference, with one parallel sequence, context length 4096, logical batch 512 and physical microbatch 128. Memory mapping remained enabled without memory locking; a 6 GiB available-memory preflight was applied before loading. These resource choices were made before formal inference.

The native chat template was rendered through the server with thinking disabled. Requests used temperature 0, seed 20261023, repetition penalty 1.0 and prompt caching disabled. Native-template prompts, request parameters and full responses are retained. Direct generation was capped at 48 tokens; checklist generation was capped at min(1024, 192 + 64m), where m is the supplied condition count. The runner rejected a request before inference if its input-token count plus output allowance exceeded 4096. It did not silently shorten inputs. Differences in output allowance reflect the different requested record sizes and are not presented as matched generation costs.

All reported seconds are wall-clock elapsed times for CPU-only execution with six inference threads, not summed CPU-core time. The shared host load was uncontrolled; external CPU competition occurred during formal checklist inference, and the frozen inference settings were retained. These measurements document the actual run and cannot support a fair efficiency ranking against earlier models or an isolated speed comparison.

JSON-constrained decoding restricted keys, state vocabulary, candidate condition IDs, candidate fact IDs and maximum array lengths. The grammar did not choose the correct state, factual entailment, complete condition coverage, appropriate request or evidence justification. Duplicate condition IDs and request IDs, unexpected keys, unknown IDs and wrong field types were checked separately; missing condition coverage was retained as an incomplete record. Invalid or incomplete records were not reclassified as semantically unknown. Unknown states were instructed to have an empty evidence list, so this deliberately concise interface does not represent competing claims or partially supporting evidence within an unresolved condition. Such source ambiguity is documented in the source audit rather than claimed solved here.

All model inputs were constructed from a whitelist of target, rule text, numbered facts and, for the checklist, numbered requirements. Expected states, requested reference IDs, source-family identifiers and administrative outcomes were withheld. Final integrity checks reconstruct each submitted user prompt and require exact agreement with the stored prompt. The archive includes the complete dynamic JSON schemas, rendered templates, raw responses, token counts and cap indicators; the lengthy schemas need not be reproduced in full here.

### S4.3 Exact natural-language instructions

The decision-only interface used the following instruction, followed by an INPUT object containing target, rule_text and facts. Its response schema had one required decision key with values supported, refuted or unknown.

> Use only the stated facts to audit the supplied rule for the named target. All rule conditions are mandatory unless an explicit alternative is given. Return supported only if the facts establish all mandatory requirements; refuted if a stated fact contradicts a mandatory requirement; otherwise unknown. One decisive contradiction remains refuted even when other facts are missing. Silence is not negation. Keep each value or attribute attached to its product, component and measurement basis. Do not infer facts from product names. Return only {"decision":"supported"}, {"decision":"refuted"}, or {"decision":"unknown"}.

The checklist interface used the following complete instruction, followed by an INPUT object containing target, rule_text, conditions and facts. A JSON FORMAT object listed each supplied condition ID with a supported|refuted|unknown placeholder and an empty evidence array, followed by a decision placeholder and an empty requests array. These placeholders specified the output schema and did not contain reference states.

> Audit whether the numbered facts establish the numbered requirements for the specified target. Use only these facts, not likely properties or background expectations. A requirement can be negative: for "no connectors", explicit absence supports it, explicit presence refutes it, and silence leaves it unknown.
> For each requirement return supported if the facts establish it, refuted if the facts explicitly contradict it, and unknown if neither. Keep attributes attached to the named product, component, operating state, and measurement basis. Facts about a different object do not establish or contradict the target requirement. Do not convert a model identifier into a physical value. Evidence must be a list of supplied fact IDs; unknown has an empty list.
> The decision is refuted when ANY mandatory requirement is refuted, even if another requirement is unknown. Otherwise it is supported only when ALL requirements are supported, and unknown otherwise. Request the IDs of unknown requirements only when the decision is unknown. Request nothing after a decisive refutation, and do not request an already established property. The supplied checklist is conjunctive; it is a provided task input, not a hidden answer.
> Return only the JSON object specified below. There is no need to explain the answer outside that object.

The deterministic comparison applies only to the returned condition states: any mandatory refutation yields refuted; otherwise all supported conditions yield supported; otherwise the state is unknown. This operation does not certify the model’s evidence. Model whole-input state, deterministic aggregate, condition states, citations and requests are scored separately. A missing unknown prerequisite and an omitted schema field are different errors: the former is a substantive evidence/request failure, while the latter is an interface-completeness failure.

### S4.4 Formal controlled inputs and paired functions

The controlled diagnostic contains 108 distinct inputs, 216 supplied conditions and twelve source families. Each family uses two explicitly supplied conditions derived from selected exclusion-clause fragments. These are artificial clauses, not the complete legal exclusions. Both interfaces receive the same complete clause meaning and identical target and facts. The checklist duplicates that meaning as condition IDs and requirements and requests a richer output. The comparison therefore changes representation, instruction and requested output jointly; it does not isolate the effect of deterministic aggregation. In particular, every-granule quantification and the wooden-frame species scope are supplied controlled interpretations, not validated legal interpretations.

Each family contains nine unique endpoints: supported base, deletion of the primary fact, irrelevant addition, decisive attribute change, retained contradiction with the companion fact omitted, original object binding, swapped object binding, equivalent expression, and evidence about another object only. Eight linked relationships per family give 96 relationships. Restoration reverses the deletion using the same base endpoint, so it adds no duplicate input. The paired observations are dependent and are not treated as 96 independent cases. Binding swaps preserve the complete value and token frequency while changing ownership. Irrelevant additions leave all relevant facts unchanged. Equivalent expressions use paraphrases or genuine unit conversions, whereas decisive changes alter the relevant attribute.

The first condition carries every whole-input state change in this particular construction. The second condition serves as a companion-state and request-suppression check; the formal whole-input reference always equals the first-condition reference. Whole-input accuracy therefore cannot establish general integration of every necessary condition. The qualification set separately includes a supported first condition and an unknown second condition, but that small check does not remove the formal design restriction. The principal functional evidence consequently comes from condition states, gap/request behavior, object binding and preservation of a verified contradiction.

Paired success requires both endpoint states to match the reference and the expected invariance or change. Always retaining an unknown answer cannot earn credit merely for stability. The report additionally records condition-state agreement; unknown-gap localization; missed necessary requests; unnecessary requests; unsupported affirmative conditions and decisions; acceptable evidence-ID sets; and preservation of a supported refutation when another condition is unknown. Direct output has no condition, citation or request fields, so those metrics are not applicable rather than assigned zero. Whole-input and paired counts are descriptive of this selected diagnostic domain, not estimates of deployment prevalence.

The refutation-with-another-unknown endpoint checks whether the whole-input answer remains refuted and the request list is empty. It does not itself certify the returned condition explanation or citation; those are scored separately.
