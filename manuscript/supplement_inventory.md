## Supplement: Structure and coverage of the exclusion inventory

### Inventory identity and purpose

The inventory contains 397 entries transcribed from a USTR index dated 17 January 2024. Its purpose is to document the range of structures present in that version and the limited intersection between the index and the historical ruling collection. It is not a sampling frame for every exclusion previously issued, a current list of legally effective exclusions, or a replacement for the candidate wording quoted in an individual ruling. The original transcription is preserved. Each audit record retains its index, rule identifier, aliases, tariff code, source wording, version and source locator. An alias or similar description is treated as a retrieval lead unless the specified matching criterion is satisfied.

All inventory indices below refer to the zero-based JSON field, from 0 to 396, rather than PDF row numbers; index 13 is therefore the fourteenth inventory record. Rule identifiers remain the primary legal-source locators.

The format distinction is consequential. There are 41 single-code entries, two entries containing date-dependent code transitions, and 354 product descriptions. A code-only entry has no additional product-description groups in this inventory, but it still has a classification gate and, where stated, a date branch. Its group count of zero therefore does not mean that no legal requirements apply. Conversely, the presence of a product description does not establish that its requirements are difficult, quantitative or fully specified.

### Reading procedure and representational granularity

Every entry was read individually by an AI research agent. The resulting silver annotations record the target level, such as article, component or assembly; visible alternatives and exceptions; measurement attributes; and references requiring external definitions. The record also separates surface structure from unresolved interpretation. Explicit row-level choices and source hashes are retained, rather than inferring a completed semantic review from a successful parser run. No legal professional independently validated these inventory labels.

A second pass selected exact source-anchored coarse requirement groups for all 354 product descriptions. Thirty initial readings used explicitly selected quotations; the remaining 324 used individually specified start anchors in the original wording. This produced 1,096 groups. The anchors were chosen after reading each scope and were not generated by counting punctuation or conjunction words. Each group has a literal quotation and character offsets. All 397 entries have a recorded coarse group count, including the 43 code-only records with a separately represented taxonomic or temporal gate.

The counting convention intentionally stops above the level of atomic legal predicates. Product categories, materials, functions, physical configurations and measurement requirements can form separate groups. A categorical alternative, constituent inventory, dimension vector for one object, or conditionally applicable standard may remain within one group. Optional modifiers can remain in that group's wording without becoming positive requirements. Across groups the reading is conjunctive, but internal alternatives, exclusions and guards retain their original scope. For example, a component list containing a burner and a stand is not counted as two independent products. This convention makes the source reading inspectable; it does not assign an ontology-independent number of necessary conditions.

Atomic condition counts, fully resolved external definitions and complete formal quantifier representations are consequently not asserted. The 1,096 total describes this particular coarse representation and should not be interpreted as a legal complexity score. It also should not be compared directly with the 208 historical condition units, which were constructed for a different collection and purpose. Inspecting the quoted groups is more informative than comparing their counts without regard to what each group contains.

### Object and logical scope

Several entries illustrate why a numerical or lexical inventory would be insufficient. The die description at index 13 limits the width or length of the sheets being processed, rather than the dimensions of the die. The alternative is retained as stated. At index 95, the motor's outside diameter, housing length and shaft length concern different objects. At index 145, input voltage and frequency are distinguished from two alternative output voltages; combining these values into a single interval would erase their roles.

Other requirements concern composition or conditional application. The trailer description at index 189 attaches compliance with ASTM F1975 to trailers designated for carrying children. It is not an unconditional requirement for every trailer. Index 195 combines three wind cups with bearings, a fan and one or more solar panels. Index 216 assigns different dimensions to two separate liner pieces. Index 218 constrains the reel carrying a lighting strip, rather than the strip itself. Index 338 uses shape-dependent dimensions for rectangular and circular tracking devices alongside shared thickness and mass limits. These readings preserve the relationships that a later executable representation would need to justify.

External identifiers require a different treatment. DIN 42802 identifies a connector standard, while the FDA Class 1 and NNI references describe a regulatory status. Their digits are not measurement thresholds. Similarly, a stated weighing capacity cannot substitute for an article's own mass. Preserving these distinctions helps identify what evidence or incorporated definitions an applicability review would need, without claiming that the inventory itself supplies them.

### Ambiguities and mechanical checks

Thirteen entries have an explicit unresolved marker for an interpretive, binding or source issue. This is a recorded review outcome, not an exhaustive estimate of ambiguity prevalence. Examples include an unclear attachment of a weight limit, an unspecified concentration basis, and a shaft measurement whose dimensional role is not named. The shelving entry at index 211 states an upper width of 183 m. The same wording was confirmed in the official PDF; it was preserved rather than silently changed to centimetres. The fireplace entry at index 155 states 5,000 BTUs without an explicit hourly basis, so the audit does not automatically treat it as power.

Mechanical validation checked 3,260 exact source spans across retrieval candidates, structural markers, coarse groups and code/date gates. It also verified record identity, source hashes and coverage links. These checks establish correspondence to the retained source, not semantic correctness or the validity of an external legal interpretation. In particular, a group can be accurately quoted while its object binding remains unresolved.

### Coverage of the ruling collection

Conservative normalized description matching linked 21 of the 86 product–exclusion pairs to 16 distinct index entries. Sixteen matched pairs belong to the 73-pair compatible-code collection. Recorded note aliases linked 11 cases and 11 entries, all within that textual intersection. Unconfirmed similar descriptions were retained separately and were not counted as matches.

Thus the confirmed intersection is 16 of 397 inventory entries, not a demonstration of model coverage across the full index. Matching also does not establish historical version equivalence. The inventory supports a transparent account of representational variety and coverage limits, while the ruling-specific clause remains the source for each historical applicability audit.
